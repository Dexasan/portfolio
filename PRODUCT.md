# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences, same pass:

- **Technical founders and early-stage startups** — looking for an infrastructure engineer who also thinks like a builder and founder. They want evidence of judgment, not just syntax.
- **Hiring managers at infra and media-tech companies** — engineering leads evaluating a junior-to-mid candidate. They're reading for depth and genuine production experience, not credentials.

Both audiences need to walk away believing the same thing: this person ships real infrastructure, not exercises. First-year university, already running production systems.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio that frames Sandesh Chapagain as a systems builder and co-founder. Not a resume with screenshots — a set of case studies that show how problems were diagnosed, why architectural decisions were made, and what the actual outcomes were.

Success looks like: a technical founder or hiring manager reading the Ditch case study and thinking "this person understands the full stack, not just their corner of it."

## Brand Personality

Technical. Curious. Builder.

The portfolio should feel like it was made by an engineer who cares how things look — not a designer cosplaying as an engineer. Quiet confidence. Nothing performed.

Voice: direct, specific, no hedging. First-person but not self-congratulatory. Writes "I built X to solve Y" not "I am passionate about Z."

## References

**Linear.app** — the primary reference. Tight spacing, opinionated decisions, nothing wasted. Every pixel feels considered. Dark, precise, no fat.

Plus moments that surprise. Not decorative — earned. The kind of creative detail that makes someone pause and look closer, then realize it also serves a purpose. The creativity is in the system, not applied on top of it.

## Anti-references

- **Design agency portfolio** — full-screen imagery, slow theatrical scroll reveals, typographic performances that look expensive but say nothing. Beautiful and empty.
- **Generic dev portfolio** — pastel project cards, GitHub star counts, "I love coffee and code" bios, identical grid of tiles.
- **SaaS landing page** — hero metrics, gradient CTAs, pricing-tier energy. This is a person, not a product launch.
- **Hacker / terminal aesthetic** — green-on-black, monospace everything, ASCII borders. The cliché of "I'm technical."

## Design Principles

1. **The work is the proof** — the portfolio is a frame for case studies, not a showcase of the portfolio itself. Every design decision defers to what serves the work.
2. **Precision with earned personality** — Linear's discipline as the baseline. Creative moments exist only when they serve understanding or reward attention. Never decorative.
3. **Show the system, not just the output** — Sandesh builds systems. The portfolio should feel like one too: consistent logic, visible craft, decisions that compound.
4. **Own the tension** — first-year university, production-grade infrastructure. Don't hide it, don't oversell it. The tension itself is the story.
5. **No performance** — no slow scroll reveals that exist to impress. If an animation or effect doesn't serve communication, it doesn't exist.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Best effort: reasonable contrast on all text, keyboard-navigable interactive elements, no motion that can't be reduced. Not a formal WCAG requirement.
